MEET

"Come to Rajiv Chowk" — and the app does the arithmetic.

Ask a friend to meet you at a station and they don't get a message to interpret. They get the decision itself: when you'd get there, when they'd get there, who waits and for how long — and, the part that actually settles it, what it costs them to say yes.

  • THE STATION Picked from where you both already are — a station on your route that they can reach.
  • THE COST Not "where", but "how much later you'd arrive". A five-minute detour is a different answer to a twenty-minute one.

PRIVACY

Your location is never written down.

Not kept briefly. Not kept anonymised. There is no table it goes into. Where you are travels straight from your phone to your friends' phones and exists nowhere afterwards — which means there is no history for anyone to ask for, leak, or sell, including us.

NEVER STORED

  • Where you are, now or ever
  • Where you have been
  • The journeys you plan or start
  • Who you met, and where

STORED, BECAUSE IT HAS TO BE

  • Your name, email and avatar from Google sign-in — so a friend has something to add you by
  • Your friendships, and which are still pending
  • Crash reports, with identifiers stripped out, deleted automatically after 90 days

HOW THAT WORKS

Live position rides a realtime broadcast channel — in-memory fan-out to the friends subscribed to it, with no database behind it. Only people you have both accepted can join your channel, and that is enforced on the server rather than by the app asking nicely.

Meet requests go further: each pair of friends gets a channel of its own, so asking one friend to meet is not readable by the rest.

Ghost Mode — you can't see friends, and they can't see you TURN OFF

Ghost Mode cuts both ways.

Once you've added someone, Routro shares your position with your friends whenever the app is open. Ghost Mode stops that — and stops you seeing them, deliberately. A switch that let you watch while hidden would be a different kind of app.

It also switches itself off when you close the app, so it can't quietly leave you invisible for a week without your knowing.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Who can see where I am?

Friends you have both accepted, and only while the app is open. Once you've added someone, sharing is on by default — that is what makes the map worth opening — and Ghost Mode turns it off in both directions at once. Nobody else can see you: the channel your position travels on is one only your friends are allowed to join, and that is checked on the server, not by the app choosing to behave.

Is my location history stored anywhere?

No — there is no table it goes into. Your position travels over a realtime broadcast channel straight to your friends and is gone. Meet requests work the same way and expire after thirty seconds. What is kept is your profile (name, email and avatar from Google sign-in, plus a short public ID), your friendships, and crash reports with identifiers stripped out — and those delete themselves after 90 days.

What is Ghost Mode?

One switch that stops your friends seeing you and stops you seeing them. It is deliberately symmetrical — being able to watch while invisible would make this a different app — and it switches itself off when you close Routro, so you can't leave yourself hidden for a week by accident.

Does following a journey drain my battery?

It uses a single low-priority foreground notification rather than constant background polling, and stops automatically when you arrive, or the moment you swipe the app away.

What happens if I close the app mid-journey?

Swiping Routro out of your recent apps ends the journey on purpose — it's the obvious, discoverable way to stop everything: the notification clears and your friends stop seeing you move.

Does it work underground, without signal?

Routing does. The whole network — every station, every track, the fare slabs — is compiled into the app, so planning a route and counting stops needs no connection at all. Your friends' positions are the part that needs one, and the app reconnects on its own when the train reaches a platform with signal.

Which metro networks does it cover?

The Delhi Metro (all lines), the Airport Express, NCR Metro in Gurugram and Noida, and the Delhi–Meerut RRTS — one graph, one app, no switching between line-specific apps.

Why is it not on the Play Store?

It will be. Until then the APK here is the whole distribution, signed with the same key every release, with a checksum published beside it. Updates after the first install arrive over the air, so this is a one-time download rather than a habit.

Which APK do I download?

The page tries to work that out for you, and gets it right often enough to lead with an answer — but Android browsers frequently don't report the architecture at all, so the full list is always one click away. If you'd rather not think about it, take the universal build: it works on everything, at roughly twice the size.

Is Routro available on iPhone?

Not yet — it's Android-only for now. The background tracking that makes live journeys work is being built as an iOS Live Activity next.
